Three ways to spend a night under the sky

Every camp includes a guide, telescopes, tents, dinner and chai at midnight. Choose by how far you want to go.

First Light

Find the Pole Star, see craters on the Moon and spot your first planet through a telescope. Back home in time for breakfast.

Length
1 night
Group size

Deep Sky Weekend

Galaxies, star clusters and glowing nebulae need a truly dark sky and some patience. Two nights gives the weather two chances to clear.

Length
2 nights
Group size
Up to 10 people
Good for
Anyone who has done First Light

Night Lens

What to pack

Nights at a dark site get colder than you expect, even after a warm day.

You bring

  • One more warm layer than you think you need
  • A water bottle and something to snack on
  • Shoes for walking on uneven ground
  • Binoculars, if you own a pair
  • Your phone, with the brightness turned all the way down

We bring

  • Telescopes, set up and aimed for you
  • Tents, sleeping mats and sleeping bags
  • Red torches, so your eyes stay used to the dark
  • Folding chairs and blankets
  • Dinner, breakfast and chai at midnight

Questions people ask before booking

Do I need to know anything about astronomy?

No. Most people on a First Light camp have never looked through a telescope. Your guide starts from the very beginning.

What happens if it's cloudy?

We check the forecast every day in the week before a camp. If it looks bad we move to the next clear weekend, or refund you in full if the new date doesn't suit you.

Can children come?

Yes, from age 10 with an adult. Night Lens camps are for ages 16 and up because they finish late.

How do I get to the site?

We email directions a week before. If you don't drive, a shared minibus leaves the city at 4 pm on the day of the camp.

Where do I sleep?

In two-person tents with proper mats and sleeping bags. Most people turn in after 2 am, and nobody gets woken before 8.
